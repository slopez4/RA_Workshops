A science workshop for the Memory and Brain Lab: **what do smell, music, food and other senses tell us about nostalgia as a memory phenomenon, and what could we study next?** The talk covers what nostalgia is, how to think about it with autobiographical-memory theory, what is known (and contested) about each sense, and where the gaps are. Then each of you pitches a study idea.

The look of the deck is a trip through childhood decades: **1970s** retro colors for what nostalgia is, **1980s** primary-color playground for the memory lens, **1990s** pastels with the five-sense icons for the senses, and a night-time **now** for methods and your own ideas.

## What you'll be able to do afterward

- Define nostalgia and say what triggers it and what it does (the regulatory model)
- Judge the evidence: average effect size, daily life vs lab, cross-cultural reach
- Explain why sensory cues are good retrieval keys and where that story gets complicated (the Proust effect)
- Tell *feeling rich* from *being rich* in a memory, and choose measures that separate them
- Spot the confounds that make cross-sense comparisons unreliable
- Read a paper with a structured template, run a small search, size a study, and write a one-page study brief

## Using the literature well

- **Open the paper before you quote a number.** Summaries here were written from abstracts, and the search was non-systematic.
- **"No study found" is not "no study exists."** Run your own search (workbook Module 4).
- The practice questionnaires in the workbook are **not validated**: use published scales in a real study.
